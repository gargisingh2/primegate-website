Italy Student Visa for Vocational Courses (2026): Requirements, Documents and Timeline

By the Primegate Consultancy Advisory Team · Last updated 17 October 2026 · 6 min read

A guide for international students and their families, from Primegate Consultancy, the appointed Exclusive Representative Office for Fondazione Doria.

A vocational course in Italy can be your fastest route to a European career, but first you need the right visa. The good news: Italy has a dedicated study visa for vocational training, and the rules are clear once you know them. This guide covers who qualifies, exactly which documents embassies ask for, how long it takes, and what life looks like on a student permit.

Can you get an Italian student visa for a vocational course?

Yes. Italy issues a national (Type D) study visa for vocational training, for courses of up to 24 months run by a training institution accredited by an Italian Region [1][2].

Italian immigration law (Article 39-bis of the Consolidated Immigration Act) allows international students to enter Italy for study purposes to attend vocational training courses. These visas are issued within a quota set by the Ministry of Labour [2]. That's one more reason to choose an accredited, authorised programme and apply early.

Who qualifies (an accredited institute and an approved course)

You qualify when you enrol on a vocational course that an Italian Region has accredited and authorised, and your background is relevant to that course [1][3].

Italian embassies check three things:

  1. The institution is accredited by the Region. Fondazione Doria is accredited by the Campania Region and recognised by the Italian Ministry of Education (MIUR).
  2. The course is authorised and lasts no more than 24 months. The embassy needs proof that the course "is among those authorized by the Region for this purpose" [1].
  3. Your profile fits the course. You show previous study or work experience that is relevant to the programme you've chosen [1][3].

Documents checklist

For a vocational training visa, you'll need your passport, course documents from the institute, proof of your qualifications, proof of financial means, accommodation and insurance [1][3][4].

Requirements vary slightly by embassy, so always check the checklist published by the Italian embassy or consulate for your country of residence.

Personal documents

  • National (Type D) visa application form, completed and signed [1]
  • Passport with at least two blank pages. Validity rules differ: one embassy asks for 15 months from the application date, another for 3 months beyond your departure from the Schengen Area [1][4]
  • Recent ICAO-format photograph [4]
  • Flight reservation. You don't need to buy the ticket before the visa decision [4]

Course documents (Primegate Consultancy and Fondazione Doria help you prepare these)

  • Proof that the institution is accredited by the Region [3]
  • Enrolment acceptance stating the course duration, attendance dates and structure [3]
  • Detailed course programme, including internship modules [3]
  • Receipt for the course enrolment fee [3]

Academic documents and the Declaration of Value or CIMEA statement

Your diploma usually needs a Declaration of Value from the Italian embassy, or a CIMEA certificate, to show what your qualification is worth [1].

  • Declaration of Value (Dichiarazione di Valore): an information document issued by Italian embassies and consulates. It describes the value of your qualification in the country where you earned it. It isn't a formal recognition of the qualification [5].
  • CIMEA statement: CIMEA is Italy's official information centre on the recognition of foreign qualifications. Embassies accept a CIMEA certificate as an alternative [1][5].
  • Work experience: instead of a diploma, some embassies accept employment contracts or payslips showing experience in the same sector as your course [1].

Proof of funds, accommodation and insurance

Fondazione Doria doesn't ask for proof of funds at admission. The Italian embassy checks your finances at the visa stage, against a benchmark of €783.06 a month (about €10,179.85 a year) [4].

ItemWhat embassies typically ask for

Step-by-step process and timeline

Allow up to three months for the embassy to process your visa, and plan your start date around it. With monthly intakes at Fondazione Doria, that's easy to do [3].

  1. Choose your programme and intake month with your Primegate Consultancy advisor.
  2. Initial counselling with the admissions team.
  3. Get your documents ready: a Declaration of Value or CIMEA statement for your diploma, translations and passport.
  4. Submit your application to Fondazione Doria.
  5. Application review by Fondazione Doria.
  6. Receive your offer letter, an institutional offer letter.
  7. Pay the programme fee according to the agreed structure.
  8. Apply for your Type D visa at the Italian embassy or consulate. Processing can take up to three months and cannot be expedited [3][6].
  9. Travel to Italy once your visa is approved.
  10. Apply for your residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) within 8 working days of arrival [7].
  11. Begin your programme, then move into your professional internship of approximately three months.

Most visa problems come from documents that don't meet the embassy's published requirements, and each one can be avoided with careful preparation.

Living in Italy on a student permit

Once you arrive, you apply for your residence permit within 8 working days, register for healthcare, and you can work part-time while you study [7][8].

  • Residence permit: apply within 8 working days of arrival [7].
  • Healthcare: study-permit holders can register voluntarily with Italy's national health service (SSN) for an annual contribution of €700 [9]. Ask your advisor which option suits your programme.
  • Part-time work: study-permit holders can work up to 20 hours a week, up to 1,040 hours a year [8].
  • Staying on to work: a valid study or vocational training permit can be converted into a work permit once you have a job offer. This can happen at any time of year, outside the annual quota system [10][11].
  • Cost of living in Angri: Fondazione Doria's campus is in Angri (province of Salerno), where rents average €5.68 per m² a month, against €10.35 across the province [12]. A monthly transport pass in nearby Salerno costs about €33 and a mobile plan about €12 [13].
