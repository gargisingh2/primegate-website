Study in Italy for International Students (2026): Vocational Courses with Internships at Fondazione Doria

By the Primegate Consultancy Advisory Team · Last updated 17 October 2026 · 7 min read

A guide for international students and their families, from Primegate Consultancy, the appointed Exclusive Representative Office for Fondazione Doria.

You don't need a long academic degree to build a career in Italy. At Fondazione Doria, you can train for an in-demand job on a hands-on vocational programme that leads into a professional internship. Programmes are available in English, and intakes run every month. This guide explains how the programmes work, what they cost, and how to get from application to internship.

Why international students choose Italy and Fondazione Doria

Italy is one of Europe's fastest-growing study destinations, with about 110,000 international students, and Fondazione Doria gives them an accredited, career-focused route in [1].

International enrolment in Italy grew 14% between 2019 and 2024 and has risen by roughly 10% a year since 2022 [1].

Fondazione Doria (Istituto Scolastico Fondazione Doria) is a training institute in Angri, in the Campania region of southern Italy. It offers:

  • Accreditation and quality: accredited by the Campania Region, recognised by the Italian Ministry of Education (MIUR), and certified to ISO 9001, 27001 and 45001.
  • Results: Fondazione Doria reports that it has trained 12,000 students and that 85% are employed within a year.
  • Teachers from industry: every course is taught by professionals still working in the sector, with labs and placements in partner companies.
  • English delivery: programmes are available for international students in English.
  • Flexible start dates: intakes every month, so you start when you are ready, not once a year.

The five programme streams

The five streams are:

  • Healthcare and Social Care (30 programmes)
  • Construction and Technical Trades (31)
  • Hospitality, Tourism and Culinary (32)
  • Fashion, Beauty and Personal Care (4)
  • IT, Digital and Automation (30)

Programmes range from Social and Healthcare Operator (OSS) and Elderly Care Operator to Pizza Maker, Pastry Chef, Welder, Plumber, Cybersecurity, Artificial Intelligence and Industrial Robotics.

Training is hands-on. You learn on working equipment in a commercial kitchen, a salon or IT workstations. Campania's hospitality, construction, healthcare and tourism employers host the internship stage.

Fondazione Doria's Region-recognised courses end with a public examination and a nationally valid certificate. The OSS course, for example, runs for 1,000 hours over 12 months, including 450 hours of supervised placement.

Good to know: Fondazione Doria decides which authorised training centre each student attends, and confirms it in writing. Programme availability depends on intake, centre capacity and regulatory approval.

Language: Italian-taught vs English-taught options

Fondazione Doria programmes are available for international students in English, and Italian language classes are included to give you an edge at work and in daily life.

You don't need to speak Italian to join, and no IELTS is required. Italian embassy checklists for vocational training visas do not ask for a language certificate either [2][3][4].

Your training is delivered in English. Alongside it, you take Italian classes from beginner (A1) to A2 level. Everyday Italian helps you settle in, connect with your internship employer and stand out when you apply for jobs in Italy.

Costs: tuition and living expenses in Angri

Fondazione Doria's programme fees run from €9,500 to €13,500, and living in Angri costs far less than most of Italy: rents average €5.68 per m² a month, about 45% below the Salerno province average [5].

Student visa for Italy: documents and timeline

You do not need proof of funds to be admitted to Fondazione Doria. Once you hold your offer letter, you apply for a national (Type D) study visa for vocational training, and processing can take up to three months [3][4].

Admission is about choosing the right programme. The financial checks come later, at the visa stage, and they are set by the Italian embassy, not by Fondazione Doria.

What Italian embassies typically ask for at the visa stage [2][3][4]:

  • Application and passport: a national visa application form and a valid passport. Check your embassy's validity and blank-page rules.
  • Course documents: proof that Fondazione Doria is accredited by the Region; your enrolment acceptance with course dates; the detailed course programme, including internship modules; and your fee receipt.
  • Your background: previous qualifications or work experience relevant to the course. Diplomas may need a Declaration of Value or a CIMEA certificate.
  • Proof of financial means: the embassy benchmark is €783.06 a month (about €10,179.85 a year). Requirements vary by embassy.
  • Accommodation: proof of where you will stay. Some embassies accept an accommodation declaration issued by the training institution.
  • Insurance: travel medical insurance covering at least €30,000.

Apply at the Italian embassy or consulate responsible for your country of residence. Your Primegate Consultancy advisor helps you prepare your file.

Your timeline with Fondazione Doria:

  1. Choose your programme and intake month based on your interests and career goals.
  2. Initial counselling with the admissions team.
  3. Submit your application and supporting documents.
  4. Application review by Fondazione Doria.
  5. Receive your offer letter, an institutional offer letter.
  6. Pay the programme fee according to the agreed structure.
  7. Apply for your visa, with visa assistance. Allow up to three months for processing [4].
  8. Travel to Italy to your designated training centre once your visa is approved.
  9. Apply for your residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) within 8 working days of arrival [8].
  10. Begin your programme.
  11. Complete your internship, a professional internship of approximately three months.

With intakes every month, you can plan your start date around your visa timeline instead of waiting for a single annual intake.

Working in Italy during and after your studies

Every Fondazione Doria vocational programme leads into a professional internship, and Italian law lets you convert a valid study or vocational training permit into a work permit at any time of year, outside the annual quota system [9][10].

  • During your studies: study-permit holders can work up to 20 hours a week, up to 1,040 hours a year [11]. Your Primegate Consultancy advisor can explain how this applies to your programme.
  • Your internship: each vocational programme leads into a professional internship of approximately three months with employers in your sector. Fondazione Doria's guidance desk helps match students with employers looking for qualified staff, and the institute reports that 85% of its students are employed within a year.
  • After your course: Italy's Ministry of the Interior confirms that holders of a valid study or vocational training permit can convert it into a work permit. You apply with your completion certificate and an employer-signed contract [10]. Since 2023, this conversion has sat outside the annual Decreto Flussi quotas, so it can happen at any time of year with no numerical limit, as long as your permit is still valid [9].
