Jobs in Italy for International Students (2026): In-Demand Careers You Can Train For

By the Primegate Consultancy Advisory Team · Last updated 17 October 2026 · 5 min read

A guide for international students and their families, from Primegate Consultancy, the appointed Exclusive Representative Office for Fondazione Doria.

Italian employers are hiring, and many of them can't find the people they need. If you're an international student looking for a practical route into a European career, the fastest path is often a vocational course in a shortage sector, followed by a professional internship. This guide shows where the demand is in 2026 and which Fondazione Doria programmes train you for it.

Why Italy is short of skilled workers

Italian businesses planned about 565,100 hires in September 2026 alone, and in 44.1% of cases they expected difficulty finding the right people [1].

Over the September to November 2026 quarter, companies planned more than 1.5 million hires [1]. The main reason roles are hard to fill is simply a lack of candidates (26.4% of planned hires), followed by candidates without the right preparation (13.6%) [1].

Three forces drive the gap:

  • An ageing population. Italy is the oldest country in the EU by share of people aged 65 and over. They now make up 25.1% of the population, almost 14.8 million people [2].
  • Openness to international workers. 23% of planned hires in September 2026 were earmarked for foreign workers [1]. The government has also set quotas for 497,550 foreign-worker entries for 2026–2028 [3].
  • A premium on practical experience. For 65.5% of planned hires, employers ask for specific professional experience [1]. That's why training with an internship matters.

Healthcare and social care (OSS, elderly care, childcare)

With a quarter of Italians aged 65 or over and 2.5 million aged 85 or over, demand for qualified care workers is structural, not seasonal [2].

Care workers support nurses and families in hospitals, residential care homes and home care services. The Social and Health Care Worker (OSS) qualification is the core credential. At Fondazione Doria, the Region-recognised OSS course runs for 1,000 hours over 12 months, including 450 hours of supervised placement, and leads to a qualification valid nationwide.

Hospitality and culinary (chef, pizza maker, pastry chef, hotel operations)

Restaurant and food-service workers were Italy's single most-hired profession in September 2026, with 66,790 planned hires in one month [1].

The hospitality sector's job vacancy rate is 4.8%, the highest in Italy and well above the 1.9% national average, according to Istat data reported by FIPE-Confcommercio. Half of all restaurateurs (51%) struggle to find dining-room and kitchen staff, and tourism and food service alone planned more than 340,000 hires between April and June 2026 [4].

Construction and technical trades (electrician, plumber, HVAC, welder)

Skilled trades and machine operators accounted for 162,720 planned hires in September 2026, nearly three in ten of all hires in Italy [1].

Industry, including construction, accounted for 26.5% of planned hires, and construction labour was the fastest-growing profession of the month [1]. Qualified installers and technicians, who can work safely on electrical, plumbing and climate systems, are the people site and maintenance companies need.

IT, AI and automation (cybersecurity, data, Industry 4.0)

Tech skills are scarce across Europe: 57.5% of EU companies that tried to hire ICT specialists in 2023 had difficulty filling the roles [5].

ICT specialists made up 5.0% of EU employment in 2025, and Italy alone employs about 0.9 million of them [6]. Italy's manufacturing base also needs technicians who can run automated lines, programme PLCs and maintain robots, the skills behind "Industry 4.0".

How job-training courses with internships lead to these jobs

Italian employers ask for specific experience on two-thirds of hires, and a vocational course with a built-in internship gives you that experience before you apply [1].

Here's how the Fondazione Doria pathway builds your employability:

  1. Train in English on real equipment. Programmes are available in English, and you learn in the same kind of kitchens, salons, labs and workstations you'll use on the job.
  2. Learn from people in the trade. Every course is taught by professionals still working in the sector.
  3. Add workplace Italian. Italian classes (A1–A2) run alongside your training, helping you communicate with colleagues and customers.
  4. Complete a professional internship. Each programme leads into an internship of approximately three months with employers in your sector, in Campania's hospitality, construction, healthcare and tourism industries.
  5. Get matched with employers. Fondazione Doria's guidance desk helps connect students with employers looking for qualified staff. The institute reports that 85% of its students are employed within a year.
  6. Move from student to worker. Under Italian law, a valid study or vocational training permit can be converted into a work permit once you have a job offer. This can happen at any time of year, outside the annual quota system [7][8].
