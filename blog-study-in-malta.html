Study in Malta: A Complete 2026 Guide for International Students

By Tejas Raju, Primegate Consultancy · Last updated 11 August 2026 · 7 min read

Malta has quietly become one of Europe's most accessible study destinations: an English-speaking EU member state, in the Mediterranean, with recognised qualifications and tuition well below the UK or US. This guide covers what it costs, whether a Maltese degree is recognised, the visa basics, and how to choose the right institution.

Key takeaways

  • Recognition: Malta is in the EU and follows the Bologna Process - qualifications use ECTS and sit on the MQF, referenced to the EU's EQF.
  • Cost: tuition roughly €4,000-€12,000/year depending on level; living €600-€1,200/month.
  • Language: English is an official language and the medium of instruction.
  • Why Malta: EU + Schengen, safe, sunny, affordable, and a gateway to Europe.

Is a Malta degree recognised internationally?

Yes. As an EU member state, Malta follows the Bologna Process, so accredited Maltese qualifications use the European Credit Transfer System (ECTS) and are recognised across the EU and internationally. Qualifications are placed on the Malta Qualifications Framework (MQF), which is referenced to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF) - so an MQF Level 6 bachelor's, for example, maps directly to EQF Level 6 elsewhere in Europe. Recognition of foreign qualifications is handled by MQRIC, the information centre under Malta's Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA).

The practical upshot: a degree earned at an MFHEA-licensed institution in Malta carries weight for further study and employment across Europe.

How much does it cost to study in Malta?

Malta is notably more affordable than most Western European destinations. Typical ranges:

ItemTypical range (per year unless noted)
Diploma / certificate tuition€4,000 - €6,000
Bachelor's tuition€6,000 - €10,000
Master's tuition€7,000 - €12,000
Living costs€600 - €1,200 / month

Specialised programmes (for example medicine or dentistry) sit well above these ranges. Living costs vary with accommodation (€300-€700/month is common), food, and transport. Always confirm current fees with the institution before you plan.

Do you need to speak Maltese?

No. English is an official language of Malta and the language of instruction at most higher-education institutions. International students can study, live and work in English, which is a big part of Malta's appeal.

Student visas and staying in Malta

EU/EEA students do not need a visa to study in Malta. Non-EU students generally apply for a national (long-stay) study visa and residence permit, supported by an acceptance letter, proof of funds, health insurance and accommodation. Requirements and processing times change, so verify the current rules with the institution and Malta's Identità (immigration) authority - or let your advisor manage it.

Why international students choose Malta

  • EU + Schengen membership and an EU-recognised degree.
  • English-speaking everyday and academic environment.
  • Safe, Mediterranean lifestyle with a large international community.
  • Affordable relative to the UK, US, Canada or Australia.
  • A genuine gateway to the wider European study and job market.
