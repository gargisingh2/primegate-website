Study Abroad in Malta vs Dubai: A Beginner's Guide for Gulf & South Asian Students

By the Primegate Advisory Team · Last updated 13 August 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Choose Malta for an EU-recognised qualification and the option to stay and work in Europe.
  • Choose Dubai to study close to home in a tax-free hub, often while working.
  • Work rights differ: the UAE now allows student work permits; Malta permits limited student work under EU rules.
  • Both are English-taught and safe - and both are served by the same IDEA education group, which runs campuses in Malta and Dubai.

Malta and Dubai come up again and again with the students and parents we advise across the Gulf and South Asia - and for good reason. Both are safe, English-friendly and far more affordable than the UK or US. But they suit different people. Here is how we help families tell them apart.

The quick version

Choose Malta if a European, EU-recognised qualification and the option to stay in Europe matter most. Choose Dubai if you want to study close to home, in a tax-free hub, ideally while working. Neither is "better" - they answer different questions.

Cost

Malta is one of Europe's more affordable destinations - tuition commonly runs a few thousand to low five-figures in euros per year, with living costs to match. Dubai's tuition varies widely by programme, and its big advantage is that flexible, part-time study lets many students earn while they learn, which changes the real cost picture.

Recognition and where it takes you

A Maltese qualification sits on the MQF, referenced to the EU's EQF, so it is recognised across Europe - useful if you are thinking about the wider European job market later. Dubai qualifications are aligned to frameworks too, and the real draw is proximity to the Gulf's employers and a fast-growing regional economy.

"I never frame it as Malta versus Dubai. I ask one question: where do you want to be working in five years? If the honest answer is Europe, Malta is the smarter bet. If it's the Gulf, Dubai gets you into that job market while you're still studying. The destination should follow the career plan, not the other way round."

— Tejas Raju, Director, Global Business Development & Strategic Partnerships, Primegate Consultancy

Language, safety and lifestyle

Both teach in English and rank as safe, welcoming places for international students. Malta offers a Mediterranean, EU lifestyle with easy travel across Europe. Dubai offers a global, multicultural city that is a short flight from home for most Gulf and South Asian families - often the deciding factor for younger students or those who want to visit family often.

Work while you study

This is where Dubai has shifted. UAE rules now let students work legally with a student work permit, which makes studying there far more financially realistic. Malta permits limited student work under EU rules. If earning during your studies is central to your plan, weigh this carefully.

Both destinations happen to be served by the same education group - IDEA - which runs campuses in Malta and the UAE, so it is possible to compare like-for-like and even move between them.
