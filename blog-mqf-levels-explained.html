MQF Levels Explained: Malta Qualifications Framework (Levels 1–8)

By Tejas Raju, Director, Global Business Development & Strategic Partnerships, Primegate ConsultancyLast updated 26 October 20268 min read

Quick answer

The MQF (Malta Qualifications Framework) is Malta's national qualifications framework, managed by the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA). It has eight levels, plus introductory levels A and B, and Levels 1–8 map directly to the same levels of the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, Level 7 a master's, and Level 8 a doctorate.[2]

Looking for Malaysia's MQF? This guide covers Malta's framework. Malaysia's is the Malaysian Qualifications Framework, run by the Malaysian Qualifications Agency.

Almost every course in Malta carries an MQF level. That number tells you how advanced the qualification is, what you need to get in, and where it leads next.

The full MQF table: Levels 1 to 8

Each MQF level from 1 to 8 corresponds to the EQF level with the same number; the qualifications below are the official examples for each level. Malta has been building this eight-level framework since 2007, and in 2009 referenced it to the EQF and to the qualifications framework of the European Higher Education Area.[2]

ECTS values are from MFHEA.[1] Below Level 1 sit introductory levels A and B, which recognise prior learning and have no EQF equivalent.[2] MFHEA classifies a programme with fewer ECTS than the typical value as an award rather than a full qualification.[1]

MQF Level 3: meaning and examples

MQF Level 3 is the level of a good secondary-school result: SEC grades 1–5, or a VET Level 3 qualification.[2] A Level 3 certificate carries about 60 ECTS.[1] It suits students finishing secondary education or adults returning to learning. It also matters later: Maltese degree programmes often ask for English and Maths at MQF Level 3 alongside a Level 4 qualification. The usual next step is Level 4.

MQF Level 4: meaning and examples

MQF Level 4 is the university-entry level: the Matriculation certificate, A-levels, or a VET diploma.[2] MFHEA lists the Advanced Diploma at 120 ECTS and the Pre-Tertiary Certificate at 30–60 ECTS.[1] In practice, Level 4 is the common entry requirement for a bachelor's degree. IDEA College Malta's BSc in Management, for example, asks for a qualification at MQF Level 4 (one A-level or equivalent) plus English and Maths at MQF Level 3.[6] IDEA College Malta offers accredited qualifications from MQF Level 4 up to Level 8.

MQF Level 5: meaning and examples

MQF Level 5 is the first level of higher education: undergraduate certificates and diplomas, foundation degrees and VET higher diplomas.[2] Typical credit values are 30 ECTS for an undergraduate certificate, 60 for a diploma and 90 for a higher diploma.[1] It suits students who want a shorter, career-focused qualification or a stepping stone into a bachelor's top-up. See the MQF 5 certificates and diplomas at IDEA College Malta.

MQF Level 6: meaning and examples

MQF Level 6 is a bachelor's degree.[2] MFHEA gives 180 ECTS as the typical size of a bachelor's degree.[1] It suits school leavers with a Level 4 qualification, and diploma holders who can join a top-up degree to complete their bachelor's. A Level 6 degree is the usual entry point for a master's. Browse the bachelor's degrees and bachelor's top-up degrees at IDEA College Malta.

MQF Level 7: meaning and examples

MQF Level 7 covers master's degrees and postgraduate diplomas and certificates.[2] Typical sizes are 90 ECTS for a master's, 60 for a postgraduate diploma and 30 for a postgraduate certificate.[1] Level 7 suits graduates who want to specialise or move into management. See the master's degrees and postgraduate certificates and diplomas at IDEA College Malta.

MQF Level 8: meaning and examples

MQF Level 8 is a doctorate, the highest level of the framework.[2] MFHEA lists both PhDs and professional doctorates here, with 180 ECTS as the typical size of a professional doctorate.[1] Level 8 suits experienced professionals and researchers, usually after a Level 7 master's. See the doctorate listed at IDEA College Malta.

MQF vs EQF vs NQF

The MQF and the EQF use the same numbers for the same levels; other countries' national frameworks (NQFs) may not, so compare by qualification type rather than by number.

MQF and the EQF

The EQF is a common European reference scale. Malta referenced the MQF to it in 2009, so MQF Level 6 equals EQF Level 6, and so on from Level 1 to 8.[2] Referencing makes Maltese qualifications easy to compare across Europe, but it is not automatic recognition: each country decides that, and Malta's recognition centre, MQRIC, assists.[3]

MQF vs the UK's qualification levels

The levels used in England, Wales and Northern Ireland also run from 1 to 8, and degrees sit at the same numbers: a bachelor's with honours at Level 6, a master's at Level 7 and a doctorate at Level 8.[4] Below degree level the two diverge. In the UK, A-levels are Level 3, while Malta places A-levels at MQF 4. The UK puts the HNC at Level 4 and the HND and foundation degree at Level 5, while Malta's undergraduate diplomas and foundation degrees all sit at MQF 5.[2][4]

MQF vs South Africa's NQF

South Africa's NQF has ten levels, so its numbers do not line up with the MQF.[5] A South African bachelor's degree sits at NQF 7, an honours degree at NQF 8, a master's at NQF 9 and a doctorate at NQF 10. Matric (the National Senior Certificate) is NQF 4, the basic requirement for bachelor's study.[5]

How to check your qualification's MQF level

Check the certificate and MFHEA's list of accredited courses first; if your qualification is not there, apply online to MQRIC for a recognition statement. MQRIC is the body within MFHEA that recognises qualifications against the MQF, and it is Malta's official ENIC-NARIC centre.[3]

  1. Look at the certificate. Qualifications from accredited institutions in Malta must show their MQF level on the certificate, and students must be told the level before the course starts.[3]
  2. Search MFHEA's list of accredited courses. If the qualification is already listed, it is recognised and you do not need to apply.[3]
  3. Apply online to MQRIC. For anything not listed, including most qualifications from outside Malta, submit an application for recognition through MFHEA's online system with your certificate and transcript. MQRIC no longer accepts applications in person or by email.[3]
  4. Pay the assessment fee. MFHEA currently lists €15 for a programme already on the MQRIC database, €30 for a new Maltese programme and €65 for a new foreign programme. A limited fast-track option is also available.[3]
  5. Download your recognition statement. It places your qualification at an MQF level, for use in study, job and permit applications.[3]

Start on the MFHEA academic qualifications page. For questions, MQRIC can be reached on Freephone 153 (from Malta) or at mqric@mfhea.mt.[3]
